\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We validate through Monte Carlo simulation ($K{=}3$), cross-$K$ robustness checks ($K{=}5,7$), and empirical validation with real LLM confusion matrices from three NLP tasks ($K{=}2,3,10$).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

For each topology, we sample 100{,}000 confusion matrices from a Dirichlet$(1,1,1)$ prior, compute analytical probability limits, and validate against Monte Carlo simulation ($N{=}10{,}000$). ASV experiments use $C(\delta) = (1{-}\delta)I + \delta C_0$.

\subsection{Bias Taxonomy}
\label{sec:taxonomy_results}

\paragraph{Theory-MC agreement.}
Analytical probability limits match Monte Carlo estimates with MAPE of 1.6\%, averaged across 161 scenarios (23 LLM configurations $\times$ 7 topologies; per-topology range: 0.3\%--3.7\%).

\paragraph{Exposure DAG: sign reversal under multi-class misclassification.}
Under realistic accuracy (diagonal $\geq 0.7$), the violation rate is 13.7\% with 2.1\% sign reversal; at diagonal $\geq 0.9$, violation persists at 11.9\% (Appendix~\ref{sec:iv_dirichlet_subspace}). Under the full Dirichlet prior, 85\% of matrices violate the binary ``toward-null'' guarantee and 83.4\% cause sign reversal; $\min_k C_{kk} < 0.55$ predicts sign reversal with 91\% accuracy (Appendix~\ref{sec:sign_determinants}). The $K{=}2$ control confirms classical attenuation~\cite{brenner1993}, establishing the multi-class failure as structurally distinct.

\paragraph{Structural guarantee for non-exposure topologies.}
Five topologies exhibit zero sign-flip rate (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}): confounding, mediation, and front-door show 100\% amplification~\cite{brenner1993, dosemeci1990} but never reverse the sign; collider is protective ($|B(C)|$ non-increasing; Conjecture~\ref{conj:monotone}); M-bias shows weakest sensitivity (53.2\% violation). All patterns persist at $K{=}5,7$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:cross_k_robustness}).

\paragraph{IV: Wald estimator instability.}
The IV topology is the sole non-exposure exception: 52.6\% of random matrices cause sign reversal, stable across $K{=}3,5,7$. Diagonal dominance ($C_{kk} > 0.5$) eliminates sign reversal but preserves amplification (Appendix~\ref{sec:iv_dirichlet_subspace}); however, all 23 empirical LLM configurations show zero IV sign reversal.

\subsection{ASV Framework Validation}
\label{sec:asv_results}

\paragraph{ASV results.}
The ASV envelope is $\varepsilon$-approximately monotonic for non-IV topologies ($\varepsilon < 0.02$ for non-exposure under moderate effects, larger for exposure; Conjecture~\ref{conj:monotone}). Figure~\ref{fig:asv_ranking} shows the vulnerability ranking: IV is maximally fragile, M-bias is immune ($\text{ASV} = \infty$). Sign-flip ASV: 83.4\% of exposure matrices yield $\delta^*_{\text{sf}} < 1$; IV ranges 1.1--6.7 (Table~\ref{tab:asv_grid}). Significance ASV exhibits a phase transition at $N^* \approx 6{,}000$--$10{,}000$; bootstrap CIs ($B{=}1{,}000$; Appendix~\ref{sec:bootstrap_asv}) confirm stable topology ranking despite wide IV intervals.

\subsection{Empirical Validation}
\label{sec:empirical}

We validate using real LLM confusion matrices from HumAID~\cite{alam2021humaid} ($K{=}10$), VAST ($K{=}3$), and CivilComments ($K{=}2$): four LLMs, 23 configurations, 161 bias scenarios.

\paragraph{Empirical bias ranking.}
HumAID ($K{=}10$) matches predictions: IV (12.4--42.1\%) and exposure (9.0--17.4\%) dominate; remaining topologies $<$15\%. $F_1$ shows no rank correlation with bias ($\rho = -0.10$, $p = 0.45$; Figure~\ref{fig:f1_vs_bias}), but per-topology patterns diverge: five topologies show $F_1{\uparrow}{\to}\text{bias}{\downarrow}$ ($\rho \leq -0.53$), while M-bias reverses ($\rho = 0.87$; Appendix~\ref{sec:within_topo_f1}). Of exposure coefficients, 14/23 (61\%) exhibit sign reversal---all secondary; zero across 138 non-exposure scenarios.

\subsection{Correction Guidance}
\label{sec:correction}

We evaluate five correction methods (naive plug-in, MLA, DSL~\cite{egami_hinck_stewart_wei2023}, MC-SIMEX~\cite{kuchenhoff2006simex}, PPI++~\cite{angelopoulos2023}) across all topologies (210 configurations; Table~\ref{tab:correction}, Appendix~\ref{app:supplementary}).

\paragraph{FWL invariance (observation).}
FWL invariance implies matrix-based methods (naive plug-in, MLA) achieve 0\% bias reduction for covariate topologies (Appendix~\ref{app:fwl_proof}); under logistic regression this breaks but ranking persists ($|\Delta| < 0.02$; Appendix~\ref{sec:fwl_nonlinear}). To our knowledge, this futility result---that matrix-based corrections cannot reduce covariate-induced bias under FWL decomposition---has not been explicitly stated in the measurement error literature~\cite{carroll2006, buonaccorsi2010}.

\paragraph{Method ranking.}
PPI++ achieves the highest RMSE reduction (47.0\% at $K{=}3$) but requires gold labels and harms M-bias ($-$88\%); MC-SIMEX is gold-free and $K$-robust (27--70\%), though it harms front-door ($-$62.4\%); rankings are stable across $K$ ($\rho \geq 0.80$; Appendix~\ref{sec:correction_k_sensitivity}).

\paragraph{Correction decision procedure.}
Given topology $\mathcal{T}$ and gold-label availability: (1)~\textbf{Risk:} M-bias $\to$ low; confounding/mediation/collider/front-door $\to$ medium; exposure/IV $\to$ high. (2)~\textbf{Method:} with gold, PPI++ except M-bias; without gold, DSL for confounding/mediation, MC-SIMEX for IV/collider. (3)~\textbf{FWL:} skip plug-in/MLA for covariate topologies (0\% reduction). If $\mathcal{T}$ uncertain, compute ASV under candidate topologies and report worst-case sensitivity (Algorithm~\ref{alg:correction}, Appendix~\ref{app:supplementary}).

\paragraph{Case studies and sensitivity.}
ClinicalBERT and HumAID ($K{=}10$) confirm all patterns---FWL invariance, PPI++ harm for M-bias/IV, partition recoverability---under linear and logistic models ($|\Delta| < 0.02$; Appendix~\ref{app:case_study},~\ref{sec:correction_k_sensitivity}). An end-to-end demo ($K{=}3$, $N{=}10{,}000$, 100 replicates; Table~\ref{tab:e2e-demo}) confirms: confounding achieves 0\% improvement (FWL), PPI++ reduces exposure bias from 42.9\% to 0.2\%, while topology-agnostic IV correction amplifies bias ($-$139\%). A semi-real study with 10 VAST matrices ($d_{\mathrm{rel}} = 0.30$--$0.38$) confirms: 93.6\% median exposure bias reduction, 0\% confounding improvement (Appendix~\ref{sec:real_cm_demo}).
